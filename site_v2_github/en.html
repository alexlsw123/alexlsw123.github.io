What the LH project taught me

Changing the network's width, depth and input length mostly made no difference. What raised accuracy was training data rebuilt point-in-time and two years of backfilled weather.

Forecast problems caught before launch. All four surfaced while replaying production.

Stopped leakage in forecast training data

A full-history cache let values from after the cutoff into training, so offline accuracy ran 17% above production.

Point-in-time training data
5 of 5
  • Rebuild the cutoff panel for every date
  • Fill gaps only from before the cutoff
  • 31,377 samples, 40 min on 4 processes
  • Replay all four periods in production
  • Spring: from trailing HGB to beating it

Filters meter spikes before forecasting

One reading 1,290× normal dragged a complex's R² to −881. Scaling now uses median and IQR, with a cap before publishing.

  • Input 1,290× normalOne complex · 2026-01-21 · skipped
  • Scaling by median and IQRInstead of mean and SD, clipped at ±8
  • January mean R² 0.781Up from −19.26 · network alone

4-bit quantization safety paper

Do NVIDIA's 4-bit checkpoints refuse the same harmful requests as the originals? I measured it on 2,014 paired English and Korean requests.

  • The chat template alone moves Korean refusals from 34.6% to 62.7%
  • 5–10% of harmful requests flip to compliance (rerun noise 0.5–2.2%)
  • Korean refusals flip 3–9 pp more often in the widest-gap model
  • Splitting across two GPUs flips up to 9% of Korean harmful requests
  • Submitted to the AAAI-27 Deployable AI workshop · first author
AAAI-27 Workshop on Deployable AI · submission
Lost in Quantization
Serving an 8B model on one consumer GPU usually requires a 4-bit checkpoint that someone else has quantized.
The chat template shipped with NVIDIA's NVFP4 release of Llama-3.1-8B raises the Korean refusal rate from 34.6% to 62.7% in BF16.
NVFP4 flips 5–10% of harmful requests from refusal to compliance.
Splitting the same checkpoint across two GPUs flips up to 9% of Korean harmful requests.

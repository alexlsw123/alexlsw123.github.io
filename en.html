LH Homeniq Apartment Load Forecasting

PeriodAug – Nov 2026 CompanyHerit RoleSole engineer: data cleaning, modeling, evaluation, serving code, handoff StackTensorFlow 2.5 · scikit-learn HGB · pandas

Problem

Forecast next-day hourly electricity use for the 77 apartment complexes in LH's Homeniq service. The first forecast is published at 06:00 the day before; a second at 02:00 on the day, using data through the previous night.

Constraints

  1. Training, inference and maintenance all on the service server itself (Python 3.7 · TensorFlow 2.5 · 8 CPU cores, no GPU)
  2. Forecast every complex, including 47 with intermittent metering
  3. Deployment bar: mean R² ≥ 0.82 across complexes, and better than the incumbent model

Chronos-2 was the most accurate model but needs Python 3.9 or later. An ONNX export was dropped because it meant adding a runtime to the server, and distillation because the teacher can't run there for retraining. Chronos-2 stayed on as the yardstick for the remaining gap.

The axis starts at 0.860. Because design and scoring used the same four periods, the final report compared all complexes, partial ones included (0.879 vs 0.881), and called the result on par.

Model changes that didn't help

  • Network width 32 · 48 · 96 · 128; number of blocks
  • Input length of 28 or 56 days (14 was best)
  • Dropout, batch size, early-stopping criteria
  • Only going from 3 to 6 seeds helped, slightly

Why weather hurt in summer

The weather history covered only a few winter months, so the model learned the heating relationship (load −0.4% per °C) and applied it backwards in the cooling season (+2.8% per °C). I switched weather off by season with a rule fixed before looking at results, validated it on unseen months, and dropped the rule once past forecasts were backfilled.

Problems caught before deployment

LEAKAGE

Offline accuracy was 17% better than production replay

Cause
Gap-filling in the full-history cache looks both ways, so values from after the information cutoff leaked into training inputs.
Fix
Rebuilt the panel for every date from only the raw data available at that time: 31,377 training samples.
Result
In spring, the blend went from trailing HGB to beating it. The same check on HGB showed no effect, so it stayed as is.
ROBUSTNESS

One meter spike drove a complex's R² to −881

Cause
A reading about 1,290× normal stayed in the inputs for 14 days and inflated the sample SD from 9 to 809.
Fix
Median/IQR scaling, winsorizing, a pre-publish cap, and a rule to skip any complex 10× off its normal level.
Result
January mean R² for the network went from −19.26 to 0.781. A bar set on the mean, not the median, exposed it before launch.
COLD START

Late meter data and short histories

Cause
Meter data arrives at different times per complex. With one shared cutoff, a replay day forecast only 29 of 77 complexes.
Fix
The delay is a model input, randomized in training. Complexes with under 14 days of history are padded by repeating the days they have.
Result
Coverage went from 74.1% to 77.4%. Most remaining exclusions have no ground truth either.
TRAINING

Three of six production models were saved mid-divergence

Cause
Corrupted samples exploded the gradients; the same seed diverged in 3 of 5 runs depending on thread order.
Fix
Training samples now pass the same input checks as serving, and each retrained model's epochs and validation loss are checked automatically.
Result
All six retrained models converged (42–67 epochs).

Lost in Quantization

The Refusals a 4-bit Checkpoint Flips and Its Chat Template Hides

PeriodOct – Nov 2026 TypeIndependent research · submitted to AAAI-27 Workshop on Deployable AI · first author StackvLLM · NVFP4 · ModelOpt · 2× RTX 5060 Ti

Running an 8B model on one consumer GPU usually means downloading a 4-bit checkpoint that someone else quantized. I asked whether a downloaded NVFP4 checkpoint refuses the same harmful requests as the original, whether that differs between English and Korean, and how reliably the difference can be measured. The study uses 2,014 paired requests, three 8B models, and two LLM judges validated against blind human labels.

  1. The chat template moves refusals more than quantization. Using the template shipped with NVIDIA's NVFP4 Llama-3.1-8B on the original BF16 weights raises the Korean refusal rate on harmful requests from 34.6% to 62.7%.
  2. Small net change, many flips. Net refusal rates move by under 5 pp, but 5–10% of harmful requests flip from refusal to compliance, against 0.5–2.2% when BF16 is simply rerun.
  3. Korean flips more. For the model with the largest English–Korean gap, Korean refusals flip 3–9 pp more often, and bilingual calibration does not reduce this.
  4. The evaluation setup is part of the result. Splitting the same checkpoint across two GPUs flips up to 9% of Korean harmful requests.
